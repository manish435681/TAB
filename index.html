High Voltage System & Train Configuration

OHE (Overhead Equipment)

OHE (Overhead equipment) is also called OCS (overhead conductor system) or OLE (Overhead line equipment) or Catenary etc. OCS may further be divided in flexible (FOCS) or rigid (ROCS).

100%
OHE STRUCTURAL ELEMENTS

Assembly Components

  • Stay Tube Comprises of a dia 38 mm tube and an adjuster to keep the bracket tube in position, insulated by a stay arm insulator.
  • Bracket tube Comprises of a dia 49 mm tube. Catenary is supported from this member by the catenary suspension bracket and clamp.
  • Register Arm Comprises of a dia 38 mm tube to register the contact wire in the desired position with the help of the Steady arm.
  • STEADY ARM ASSEMBLY Made of Al alloy to register the contact wire to the required stagger and take the push up; it is always in tension.

OHE (Overhead Equipment) Specifications

Messenger Wire/Catenary wire

  • Made of copper cadmium alloy of 19 strands each of 2.10 mm diameter, representing a total cross section of about 65 mm2.

Contact Wire

  • Hard drawn grooved copper contact wire with flat bottom of 150 mm2 cross-section.

Clearance & Connections

  • The minimum height of contact wire at support is 5.00m for regulated OHE & 5.20m for depots and inspection pits.
  • The single-phase AC 25KV high voltage connects to the primary winding of the main transformer through the VCB via the pantograph.

RETURN CONDUCTOR & OPC

Conductor systems and induction minimization mechanisms.

RETURN CONDUCTOR

  • All aluminium conductor consisting of 19 strands each of 3.99 mm diameter, with a nominal cross section of 233 sq.mm.
  • To minimize Electro magnetic induction on nearby telecom utilities, the BT/RC system is used.

OPC

  • An ACSR conductor, consisting of 7 Steel wires and 12 Aluminium wires (each 2.5mm diameter), with a nominal cross section of 93.3 Sq.mm.

Static Inverter (SIV)

Purpose & Working Principle

The Static Inverter (SIV) functions as a primary component of the train's Auxiliary Power System. It converts single-phase AC power received from the main transformer's tertiary winding into stable AC and DC power supplies required for various train auxiliary loads.

Parameter Value Description
Input Power 373 / 336V AC Single-phase power from main transformer.
Main Output 415V AC (3Φ) Constant 50Hz output for auxiliary loads.
Regulation 19kV to 27.5kV Controlled despite catenary variations.
Internal Logic AC ➔ DC ➔ AC Converter ➔ Filter Capacitor ➔ Inverter.

DC POWER NETWORK

Battery System & Charger (BCG)

Provides emergency power, control network voltage (DC110V), and train startup functionality across the vehicle network.

DC110V Bus Supply Modes

The DC power network operates using three primary bus lines: Permanent Bus, Prepared Bus, and Negative Bus.

BCG Redundancy

One healthy Battery Charger (BCG) can independently support all vehicle DC110V loads across the train if a single BCG fails.

Emergency Backup Capability

In case of a total BCG failure, the battery system guarantees emergency power for at least 60 minutes .

BRAKING

Brake System - Overview

Features a microprocessor controlled pneumatic single pipe friction brake system with Electro-Pneumatic control.

1. To stop a moving train at desired location.
2. To slow down the speed of train.
3. To stop the train to avoid a miss happening in emergency.
4. To stable a vehicle or to prevent its rolling back.
BRAKING

Brake System Categorization

1. Based on Implementation
1 Electro-Pneumatic (Friction) Brake
2 Electro-Dynamic (Regenerative) Brake
2. Based on Application
i. Service Brake Blending between friction brake and ED-brake (Load & Jerk corrected, slip Slide Protection).
ii. Emergency Brake EP brake, Load corrected, slip Slide Protection.
iii. Parking Brake Pressure release-spring actuated.
iv. Holding Brake Comes into effect near Zero speed.
EP BRAKE

Electro-Pneumatic (Friction) Brake

System Definition
An Electro-Pneumatic (EP) brake is a modern train braking system that combines high-speed electronic signal control with compressed air pressure to apply mechanical friction brakes.
Instant Response
Transmits electronic commands instantly across the trainset, eliminating pneumatic propagation delays.
Seamless Blending
Automatically supplements electro-dynamic braking effort during stopping phases or high demand.
Uniform Application
Ensures simultaneous braking across all cars to minimize coupler stress and maximize passenger comfort.
Anti-Skid Safeguard
Direct interface with Wheel Slide Protection (WSP) dynamically adjusts pressure during low adhesion.
ED BRAKE

Electro-Dynamic (Regenerative) Brake

System Definition
An Electro-Dynamic (ED) brake is a modern regenerative braking system that uses a train's electric traction motors to slow the vehicle down. When braking is requested, the electric motors run in reverse to act as generators, converting the train's kinetic energy into electrical power.
Energy Saving
Regenerated electricity is fed back to the overhead power lines, significantly reducing total power consumption.
First Choice
Serves as the primary deceleration method to minimize wear and extend the lifespan of mechanical friction pads.
Smart Blending
Automatically integrates and blends with friction brakes whenever additional stopping force is required.

2. Based on Application

i. Service Brake

The service brake is the main, fully controllable braking system used during everyday train driving to slow down smoothly or bring the train to a complete stop at stations.

Service Brake Characteristics

Characteristic Specification Details
Deceleration Rate Delivers a target average deceleration of at least 0.92 m/s² for reliable stopping.
Jerk Limitation Keeps changes in acceleration capped at 0.75 m/s³ to guarantee passenger comfort.
Response Speed Achieves full braking application within 1.9 seconds, featuring a 0.4-second delay and a 1.5-second pressure build-up time.
Priority Blending Uses electric dynamic braking on motor cars first, adding electro-pneumatic friction brakes across axles only when extra stopping force is needed.
Load Adjustment Monitors air spring suspension levels continuously to adapt braking pressure to actual passenger weight.

2. Based on Application

ii. Emergency Brake

The emergency brake is an independent, fail-safe braking system that applies maximum friction stopping power immediately to bring the train to a complete stop during dangerous or critical situations.

  • Deceleration: ≥ 1.18 m/s² avg (1.3 m/s² max) via friction braking only.
  • No Jerk Limit: Bypasses comfort controls for instant maximum force.
  • Response Time: 1.5s total (0.2s delay + 1.3s pressure build-up).
  • Irrevocable: Locks until zero speed, trigger clears, controller set to Bmax, and reset pressed.

Emergency Brake Initiation Triggers

Trigger Category Initiation Condition
Manual Controls TBC in "EM" position or Emergency Stop Pushbutton pressed.
Driver Safety Deadman switch released for > 3 seconds while moving.
Automatic Control ATP/VATC trips (overspeed, signal overrun, transmission failure).
Pneumatic Drop Main Reservoir (MR) pressure drops below 6.0 bar.
Electrical Loops Loss of 110V DC, emergency loop de-energized, or sleep mode.
Safety Incidents Detrainment door opened, mode switched to OFF/Standby running, or unauthorized towing movement.

2. Based on Application

iii. Parking Brake

A mechanical safety brake using powerful internal springs to keep the train securely parked and stationary when unpowered or unattended.

Parking Brake Characteristics

Characteristic Specification Details
Spring-Applied Applies holding force automatically via internal springs when air vents.
Air Release Requires active compressed air to compress springs and release the mechanism.
Gradient Hold Secures a fully loaded train (AW3) on a 4% gradient, even if one car fails.
Auto Air-Loss Applies automatically if pressure drops below 3.8 bar; releases above 4.8 bar.
Independent Control Functions mechanically without electronic EBCU or wheel slide protection.

2. Based on Application

iv. Holding Brake

An automatic stopping feature that keeps the train stationary at stations or on slopes when it slows down to a complete standstill, preventing it from rolling backward.

Holding Brake Characteristics

Characteristic Specification Details
Activation Speed Applies automatically when train speed drops below 0.5 km/h with a braking demand signal active.
Applied Force Level Delivers 70% of full service brake force by default, or higher if required by the brake demand signal.
Gradient Protection Holds the train in place on incline tracks until the traction system builds enough powering torque.
Automatic Reset Releases automatically once traction effort is established and train speed reaches 12 km/h.
Driver Control Override Allows manual compulsory release or override directly via the Train Control Management System (TCMS) screen.
BECU SYSTEM

BECU System

Core Takeaways & Architectural Highlights

Centralized Brake Control: Integrates Electro-Pneumatic (EP) and Electro-Dynamic (ED) braking, holding brake routines, and wheel slide protection via RACK1/RACK2 dual CAN bus and direct TCMS communication.
Intelligent Force Blending: Prioritizes ED braking to optimize energy efficiency and wear, dynamically blending EP force during stopping phases or low-adhesion conditions.
Fail-Safe Emergency Protocols: Guarantees deterministic 1.3 m/s² emergency deceleration via direct EP valve de-energization.
Advanced WSP & Active Diagnostics: Protects wheelsets via individual axle speed monitoring, automatically issuing ED Cut-Out (EDCO) when sliding exceeds thresholds, backed by RS485 diagnostic logging and control redundancy.
BCU SYSTEM

BCU System

Command Translation: Converts electronic control signals directly into physical pneumatic pressure for each bogie.
Pressure Amplification: Uses solenoid valves and high-capacity relay valves to achieve rapid cylinder pressure response times.
Load Sensing: Continuously reads air spring suspension levels to dynamically adjust braking force to passenger weight.
Safety Override: Automatically defaults to maximum load pressure calculation if suspension feedback signals are lost.
Direct Emergency Activation: Bypasses electronic processing during safety loop de-energization for instant emergency braking.
Mechanical Fail-Safe Routing: Routes direct, un-jerk-limited pneumatic pressure to brake cylinders via double check valves.
Fault Isolation: Controls each bogie independently so a failure on one bogie does not compromise the other.
Diagnostic Monitoring: Feeds real-time pneumatic pressure data back to central train control for fault detection and logging.

Intelligent Braking

Brake Blending Concept

An intelligent system that automatically combines electric dynamic (ED) and mechanical friction braking (EP) to deliver precise stopping force, save energy, and reduce wear.

Priority Execution Prioritizes Electro-Dynamic (ED) regenerative braking to maximize power savings.
Friction Supplementation Automatically adds Electro-Pneumatic (EP) friction braking to make up for any shortfall in ED effort.
Load Adjustment Dynamically calculates and adjusts braking force based on passenger weight and train speed.
Low-Speed Transfer Smoothly shifts entirely to friction brakes when speed drops below 8 km/h.
Jerk Control Manages brake handovers carefully to prevent sudden jolts and ensure passenger comfort.
Brake Blending

Brake Control Unit (BCU)

Component & Valve Overview

The B03 Manifold represents the core pneumatic logic block of the braking system.

Key Manifold Components

Pneumatic Interfaces

R1 / R Main Reservoir Supply: High-pressure air input.
T Air Spring (AS) Port: Input for load weighing pressure.
C Brake Cylinder (BC) Port: Output to apply friction brakes.

Active Control Valves

a Service Brake Block: Proportional valves for normal stops.
e Emergency Valve: Fail-safe solenoid for emergency trigger.
f Emergency Proportional Valve: Blends AS pressure during EB.
g Double Check Valve: Resolves Service vs. Emergency signals.

Power Amplification

d Relay Valve (Kr6-AA): The heart of the BCU. It takes low-volume control signals from valves a or f and outputs high-volume MR air directly to port C.

Sensors & Isolation

h/j/k Pressure Transducers: Converts air pressure to electrical feedback.
B04.x Isolation Cocks (BIC): Manual valves to cut off air.

Service Brake Control Loop

Pneumatic Flow Sequence

■ Supply ■ Control ■ Load Weighing ■ Brake Output

Airflow Path Explanation

1. Main Supply Air (Blue)

Air from the main reservoir enters the system and waits at the inlet valves.

2. Valve Activation (Green)

Solenoids receive a command. They energize, opening the path for the supply air to become control air.

3. Load Adjustment (Purple)

Air Spring (AS) pressure enters the Kr6-AA relay valve, providing passenger weight data.

4. Final Application (Red)

The relay valve blends the control air and AS pressure, sending the final Brake Cylinder (BC) pressure.

Emergency Brake Control Loop

Fail-Safe Pneumatic Sequence

■ De-energized ■ Supply ■ E-Control ■ Output

Fail-Safe Action Path

1. Loop Drop (Blue Lightning)

The electrical emergency loop is broken, causing the solenoid valve to instantly de-energize to its fail-safe state.

2. Direct Supply (Blue)

Main reservoir supply air flows directly through the de-energized paths, bypassing normal service controls.

3. Load Reference (Purple)

Air Spring (AS) pressure enters the emergency proportional valve to provide train weight data.

4. E-Control Signal (Yellow)

The proportional valve blends the supply and AS pressure, sending an emergency control signal to the relay valve.

5. Max Application (Red)

The relay valve irrevocably outputs the maximum required Brake Cylinder (BC) pressure to stop the train.

Working of Parking Brake

Dual-Coil PBMV Operation

■ MR Supply ■ Release Working ■ Apply Working ■ Feedback

B09 Valve Working Principle

1. Standby Supply (MR)

Main Reservoir (MR) air constantly supplies port P of the Parking Brake Magnet Valve (B09), waiting for an electrical command to actuate the brake.

2. Working: Brake Release (Blue)

Magnet Valve I energizes and Valve II de-energizes. The mechanical spool shifts right. Air flows from port P to B, inflating the cylinders and forcing the heavy mechanical springs to compress. The train can now move.

3. Working: Brake Application (Red)

Magnet Valve I de-energizes and Valve II energizes. The spool shifts left, cutting off MR air. Cylinder air exhausts through port S. Without air pressure, the springs aggressively expand, locking the wheels.

4. Operational Feedback

Pressure switches (B10) monitor the working state. A pressure reading above 5.2 bar confirms a successful release, while a drop below 4.0 bar confirms a successful application.
